Recent auditsThree I ran this week

Public-side observations, not paid engagements. Names anonymized.

Luxury Buckhead brokerage, $2M+ tier
Lowest-scoring check: pricing & commission transparency (0/3)
No price-band or service-tier framing anywhere on the agent page. Every price-shopper inquiry routes to a live qualifying call before they ever see a number, including the $400K starter-home shoppers who’ll never close at this tier.
What I’d build: a single “I work primarily in the $1M+ tier” line plus a two-question pre-qualifier (price band + pre-approval) on the inbound form, so sub-$1M inquiries self-deselect before they touch operator time.

Independent residential+commercial DeKalb-Fulton agency
Lowest-scoring check: qualification (0/3)
Every inbound form is 4 fields (name, phone, email, message) with zero qualifying questions. A residential buyer and a commercial tenant looking at 3,000 sqft of retail land in the same intake, get the same questions, and get sorted by whoever reads the inbox first.
What I’d build: a split inbound router with a residential tree (price band + pre-approval + timeline + buyer-or-seller) and a commercial tree (lease-or-purchase + sqft band + use-case + timeline), each SMS-backing the prospect inside 60 seconds.
Audited 2026-05-18
Compass-affiliated Buckhead luxury solo agent
Lowest-scoring check: qualification (1/3)
The only qualifier across four entry points is a single “Interest” dropdown on the legacy site, and the agent-page CTA is even thinner (name plus message). Every late-night inquiry pulls a human into a comp-pull before anyone knows if the prospect is pre-approved or just browsing.
What I’d build: a personal pre-approval + budget pre-qualifier layer that fires before the corporate auto-reply, routes hot leads straight to mobile with a transcript, and parks tire-kickers in a tagged nurture queue.
