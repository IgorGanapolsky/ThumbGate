# Employment IP hygiene (public, generic)

> **Not legal advice.** Operators and contributors who are employed by third
> parties must follow their own employment, confidentiality, and invention-
> assignment agreements. This note is product hygiene for the public ThumbGate
> project only.

## 1. Do not import employer confidential information

Never add to this repository, marketing, case studies, lesson DB, or prevention
rules any material that is confidential to an employer or client, including:

- Nonpublic source code, architecture, or internal tooling
- Internal prompts, skills, eval sets, or safeguards
- Customer/prospect identities, failures, metrics, or screenshots
- Roadmaps, incident lessons, or “anonymized” writeups whose insight came from
  confidential work

Public knowledge (published papers, public docs, open-source) is fine.

## 2. Invention assignment is not cured by “nights and weekends”

Many employment agreements assign rights in inventions created during
employment if they relate to the employer’s business or anticipated R&D, even
when built on personal equipment and personal time. Agent-governance products
can fall inside that scope for employers whose business is AI/agentic software.

**Implications for this project:**

- Prior, pre-employment public history remains the baseline provenance story.
- Net-new features that closely track an employer’s agent platform may create
  assignment risk for an employed operator — that is a matter for the operator’s
  employment counsel, not something this repo can waive.
- Active consulting sold while employed can raise separate conflict / outside-
  activity issues under some agreements.

## 3. Project hygiene

- Do not commit private employment agreement analyses, Exhibit A drafts naming
  an employer, or personal legal correspondence to this public repository.
- Prefer personal equipment and personal accounts for contributions when an
  employer agreement forbids use of employer resources.
- Do not install or incorporate ThumbGate into an employer’s systems in a way
  that grants unexpected licenses unless counsel has approved.
